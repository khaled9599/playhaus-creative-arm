# Creative System

## Content ecosystem
### Experience
Show what being there feels like. Real groups, atmosphere, food landing, reactions, game escalation and venue use.

### People
Game Masters, customers, regulars, creators, friend-group archetypes, staff personalities and recurring characters.

### Play
Games as entertainment, challenges, mood recommendations, competitions, group dynamics, discovery and prescriptions for different nights.

### Culture
Friendship, dating, group chats, indecision, phones, weekend culture, competitiveness, bad losers, cheaters, late arrivals and relatable group dynamics.

## Visual rules from approved working direction
- No white backgrounds or white text-background panels.
- White only for typography and scribbles.
- Keep scribble colors saturated and high-contrast.
- Text must not overlap faces.
- Avoid oversized text blocks at the top of imagery.
- Use a confident font mix: headline display typography plus handwritten supporting type, with a third typeface only when necessary.
- Board game photos supplied as references must not be reused as-is. Generate original scenes and use the references to make game types/components plausible.
- Prefer people genuinely interacting with the game and each other rather than posed lifestyle imagery.

## Review questions
1. Does this communicate why the night is worth leaving home for?
2. Is there a human behavior or social truth at the center?
3. Could another cafe post the same thing unchanged?
4. Does the joke feel natural rather than written by a brand trying to sound young?
5. Is the game correct enough to survive scrutiny from someone who knows it?
6. Is the execution recognizable as Playhaus before reading the logo?
